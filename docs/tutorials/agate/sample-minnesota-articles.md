# Minnesota sample articles

These fictional articles provide consistent source material for the tutorials.
The Minnesota places and counties are real. The people, organizations, events,
quotes, and statistics are invented for demonstration.

Each article includes several locations and people so you can test extraction,
geocoding, Stylebook matching, metadata, review, and search. Some people appear
in more than one article, which makes the collection useful for testing
canonical records and mentions.

Copy the full text of one article into the **Text Input** node when a tutorial
asks for sample content.

## Duluth opens neighborhood cooling rooms

```text
Duluth opens neighborhood cooling rooms ahead of August heat

By Nora Bell
July 28, 2026

DULUTH, Minn. — The City of Duluth will open three neighborhood cooling rooms this weekend as forecasters expect several days of unusually high temperatures across northeastern Minnesota.

The rooms will operate at the fictional Harborview Community Center in Lincoln Park, the fictional North Hills Library and the fictional Lakeside Family Center. They will offer water, seating and charging stations from noon to 8 p.m. Friday through Monday.

Maya Chen, director of the fictional Minnesota Community Resilience Office, said Duluth's plan is part of a pilot program focused on communities near Lake Superior.

"Heat can be dangerous even in places known for cool summers," Chen said. "The goal is to give residents a nearby place to rest before a health concern becomes an emergency."

St. Louis County will provide shuttle rides from several senior housing buildings. Fictional county health coordinator Andre Wallace said residents should call 911 for medical emergencies rather than traveling to a cooling room.

Duluth Mayor Celia Hart, a fictional official, said the city will decide next month whether to make the program permanent. The city will track attendance but will not ask visitors for identification.

The National Weather Service office in Duluth is not affiliated with this fictional program. All program details, organizations, officials and quotations in this article are invented for tutorial use.
```

## Moorhead approves a floodplain park

```text
Moorhead approves floodplain park along the Red River

By Nora Bell
July 30, 2026

MOORHEAD, Minn. — The Moorhead City Council voted Tuesday to turn 18 acres of frequently flooded land near the Red River into a public park designed to hold water during spring floods.

The fictional Riverbend Commons project will include walking paths, native prairie plantings and a small outdoor classroom. Construction is expected to begin next spring.

Fictional council member Jamal Reed said the project gives the land a useful purpose without adding homes or businesses in an area with repeated flood damage.

"This is infrastructure, but it is also a neighborhood space," Reed said. "Most of the year, families will see a park. During a major thaw, the same land can give the river more room."

Clay County will maintain an emergency access road on the western edge of the site. Maya Chen of the fictional Minnesota Community Resilience Office said the state pilot program will contribute $1.2 million in invented grant funding.

Several residents asked the city to add more lighting and preserve a row of mature cottonwood trees. Parks director Lena Voss, a fictional employee, said a revised design will return to the council in September.

Moorhead sits across the Red River from Fargo, North Dakota. The locations are real, but the project, people, funding and quotations in this article are fictional.
```

## Rochester extends late-night bus service

```text
Rochester extends late-night bus service on two routes

By Caleb Young
August 1, 2026

ROCHESTER, Minn. — Rochester will extend bus service until 1 a.m. on two routes beginning Monday in a six-month test aimed at hospital, restaurant and hotel workers.

The fictional NightLink service will connect downtown Rochester with neighborhoods in the northwest and southeast parts of the city. Buses currently stop running on those routes at 10:30 p.m.

Elena Ortiz, director of the fictional Minnesota Mobility Partnership, said shift workers helped design the schedule.

"A route is only useful when it runs at the time someone needs it," Ortiz said. "Workers told us that the trip home, not the trip to work, was the hardest part."

Olmsted County will pay half of the invented $480,000 pilot cost. The City of Rochester and a fictional coalition of downtown employers will cover the remainder.

Fictional transit manager Priya Nand said riders will pay normal fares and can request stops through the existing mobile app. The city expects about 140 passenger trips each night.

The council will review ridership, missed-trip reports and operating costs in January before deciding whether to continue the service. Rochester and Olmsted County are real places; NightLink, the organizations, people, statistics and quotations are fictional.
```

## Bemidji launches a mobile health clinic

```text
Mobile clinic will visit rural communities near Bemidji

By Caleb Young
August 2, 2026

BEMIDJI, Minn. — A new mobile health clinic will begin making weekly stops in four rural communities in Beltrami County this month, according to the fictional North Woods Health Collaborative.

The clinic will offer blood-pressure checks, vaccinations, basic laboratory tests and referrals. Its first route will begin in Bemidji before stopping in Blackduck, Kelliher and Tenstrike.

Dr. Leona Sato, the collaborative's fictional medical director, said the program is intended to supplement local clinics rather than replace them.

"A forty-minute drive can become a major barrier when a patient needs a simple follow-up visit," Sato said. "We want to bring routine care closer to where people already live."

Beltrami County will provide parking space and help residents make appointments. Walk-in visits will be available when the schedule allows.

Maya Chen, director of the fictional Minnesota Community Resilience Office, said an invented state grant will fund the first year of service. The collaborative will publish monthly information about visits but will not release patient names or medical records.

The first Bemidji stop is scheduled for Aug. 12 at the fictional Pine River Community Hall. The Minnesota locations are real; the clinic, organizations, people, grant, schedule and quotations are fictional.
```

## Mankato converts a vacant lot into gardens

```text
Mankato students turn vacant lot into neighborhood gardens

By Nora Bell
August 3, 2026

MANKATO, Minn. — Students from two Mankato high schools are converting a vacant city-owned lot into community gardens and an outdoor classroom.

The fictional Prairie Block Garden will occupy one acre near downtown Mankato. Plans include 36 garden plots, a rainwater collection system and raised beds designed for gardeners who use wheelchairs.

Fictional science teacher Marcus Hill said students spent the spring testing soil and interviewing nearby residents.

"The students learned that a public project starts with listening," Hill said. "The final design looks different because neighbors asked for shade, wider paths and space to grow food together."

The City of Mankato approved a five-year lease for the site. Blue Earth County will provide compost through an invented waste-reduction program.

Sofia Alvarez, a fictional senior at Mankato East High School, said students will grow tomatoes, beans and traditional Dakota corn in the demonstration beds. Produce from those beds will be donated to a fictional neighborhood food shelf.

Construction is expected to finish in October, with the first full planting season beginning next spring. Mankato and Blue Earth County are real places; the project, people, school activity and quotations are fictional.
```

## Twin Cities libraries share weekend delivery service

```text
St. Paul and Minneapolis libraries test weekend book delivery

By Caleb Young
August 4, 2026

ST. PAUL, Minn. — Public library systems in St. Paul and Minneapolis will test a shared weekend delivery service this fall, allowing patrons to request books from either city and pick them up closer to home.

The fictional MetroShelf pilot will begin at four library branches in Ramsey County and four branches in Hennepin County. Deliveries will run on Saturdays and Sundays for twelve weeks.

Elena Ortiz, director of the fictional Minnesota Mobility Partnership, said the project uses cargo vans that would otherwise sit idle on weekends.

"This is a small test of how public services can share transportation," Ortiz said. "A reader should not need to understand two delivery systems just to borrow a book."

Fictional St. Paul librarian Theo Grant said patrons will use their existing library cards. Requests placed by Thursday evening should arrive at the selected branch by Saturday afternoon.

Minneapolis resident Amina Yusuf, a fictional participant in the pilot planning group, asked the libraries to include large-print books and materials in Somali, Hmong and Spanish.

Officials will compare delivery costs, wait times and patron feedback before considering a permanent program. St. Paul, Minneapolis, Ramsey County and Hennepin County are real; the pilot, people, organizations, statistics and quotations are fictional.
```

## What the collection tests

- **Repeated people:** Maya Chen, Elena Ortiz, Nora Bell and Caleb Young appear
  in multiple articles.
- **Known places:** Each story names Minnesota cities and counties that can
  match records in the Tutorial Stylebook.
- **Geography:** The stories include city, county, state and regional references
  for extraction and geocoding.
- **Metadata:** The collection spans climate resilience, transportation, health,
  education, food access and libraries.
- **Editorial review:** Fictional institutions and programs create useful new
  records without mixing tutorial content with real client data.
